# Testing and QA

Automated tests, browser checks and human playtests. Fairness testing is described in GAMEPLAY_BALANCING.md. *Section numbers (§) are shared across all project documents; the index is in the README.*

## 7 Tests

- **Engine invariants** (property-based tests): no negative stocks; no troops from nowhere; transfers respect stock and capacity; movement follows the network; battles conserve men (killed + wounded + prisoners + survivors = start); invalid orders are rejected cleanly; save → load reproduces identical state.
- **Determinism:** golden replays plus a cross-browser check (Chromium, Firefox, WebKit) of the same seed giving the same hash.
- **Resumption tests:** save and reload at random pause points, including mid-battle, and confirm the final state matches an uninterrupted run (§3.4).
- **Browser tests (Playwright):** full playthrough, battle, report, reload mid-turn and mid-battle, corrupted-save recovery, share link, phone/tablet/desktop screenshots, reduced motion.
- **Accessibility:** axe checks; keyboard play of the whole Challenge, including a list-based alternative to the map; contrast; pattern + icon alongside colour.
- **Performance:** scripted pan/zoom FPS measurement; bundle-size budget check.
- **Human playtests:** I'll give you a one-page script (think-aloud play, an observation sheet, 6 questions afterwards). **Round 1: 2 testers, ~Day 15. Round 2: the other 2, ~Day 19**, so the second round tests the fixes with fresh eyes. The playtest summary screen records completion time and decisions for each tester (§1.2). I won't invent feedback; only what you report counts. Before those rounds, a short targeted playtest answers the third review's questions about Stand, retreats and the verdict ([script](playtests/2026-11-03-targeted-playtest.md)).

## Timeline tests (§3.4a)

- **T1** A delivery that arrives in a sub-step can't be used for marching or fighting during that sub-step; it is on hand from the end of the sub-step.
- **T2** Consumption never exceeds stock on hand; every shortfall is recorded and has effects.
- **T3** A force in battle neither moves nor forages during the sub-steps the battle occupies.
- **T4** No force belongs to two encounters in the same sub-step.
- **T5** Ammunition spent in one round is gone for the next, and is replenished only as §3.4a allows.
- **T6** Battle rounds advance the clock: a battle that starts in an afternoon sub-step has at most 3 rounds before nightfall.
- **T7** A battle that continues overnight across the turn boundary locks its forces out of new movement orders and resumes at dawn.
- **T8** Men, horses, wagons and supplies are conserved across every stage.
- **T9** Fatigue rises and recovers on the timeline: marching raises it within a sub-step, and night rest lowers it.
- **T10** Saving and loading at every stage, including the night phase and mid-battle, gives an identical final state. *Covered today:* engine states serialised to JSON at every pause, mid-battle included, then replayed. *Not yet covered:* browser saves (not built, §3.5), reload in a browser, checksums and corrupt saves, and resuming from internal stages between pauses.

## Frame rate (M2, V2; measured 4 November 2026)

**The probe:** `tools/measure-fps.js`, pasted into the browser console on the game page with the map showing. It drives the map with synthetic input and measures the time between animation frames, phase by phase:
- still, on the whole theatre;
- zooming in to the closest view (Ctrl + wheel, as a trackpad pinch);
- dragging the map in a slow circle at the local zoom;
- zooming out to the operations zoom and dragging there;
- zooming out to the whole theatre;
- playing the last turn again.

It counts the layers drawn before it starts and leaves the map on the whole theatre. A window that is hidden or behind another gets about one frame a second; the probe then reports itself as `throttled`, and such a run measures nothing.

**Where:** the production build (`vite build`, served by `vite preview`) in the app's built-in browser pane on your Mac. The map was 661 CSS pixels wide at a device pixel ratio of 2, on a 60 Hz display, so 60 fps is the ceiling.

| Run | Layers drawn | Median fps, every phase | Slowest 5% of frames | Frames over 25 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed 31, turn 4, a battle waiting | 5 groups, 4 enemy reports, 5 supply lines, 8 foraged districts, dust on 7 roads, a battlefield, 5 trails | 59.9–60.2 | 56.8–59.2 fps | 1 of 953 |
| Seed 31, turn 2, raining | rain falling, mud on all 28 roads, 5 groups, 1 enemy report, 5 supply lines, 2 districts, 5 trails, 5 planned routes | 59.9 | 57.5–59.5 fps | 0 of 951 |

**V2 is met at this size:** every phase holds a median of 60 fps, above the 55 fps asked, and the PixiJS trigger (under 50 fps, §3.1) is far off. Rain and dust cannot be drawn together (dust rises only on dry roads, and rain turns every road to mud), so "all layers on" was measured as these two runs.

**One run is not in the table:** an earlier rainy run held 60 fps for its first half, then fell to a 30 fps median, with the slowest frames at 20 fps, from the zoom-out onwards. A rerun under the same conditions held 60 fps throughout. The cause was not found. The app recorded a click in the page just before that run, and other load on the Mac is possible.

**With the painted terrain (4 November, the same game, seed 31, turn 4, with the relief, 25 river lines and the hachures):**
- **The first try failed the target:** zooming in fell to a 20 fps median, and dragging at the operations zoom to a steady 20 fps. Every hachure was drawn as a non-scaling stroke, recomputed on every frame.
- **The fix:** only the hachures in and around the view are drawn, sized with the zoom.
- **After the fix, a run started with the pane on screen:**

  | Phase | Median fps | Slowest 5% | Frames over 25 ms |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Still, whole theatre | 59.9 | 57.8 | 0 of 121 |
  | Zooming in to local | 59.9 | 58.8 | 0 of 150 |
  | Dragging, local | 59.9 | 58.1 | 0 of 150 |
  | Zooming out to operations | 59.9 | 58.1 | 0 of 73 |
  | Dragging, operations | 59.9 | 59.5 | 0 of 151 |

- **Not counted:** the pane went hidden during the last two phases, zooming out to the theatre and playing the last turn, so they measure nothing.

**Not measured:**
- **A full-size window:** emulating a window larger than the pane throttles its frames, so the probe could not run there. One run of the probe in a full-size browser window is still to do.
- **A display above 60 Hz.**
- **Seams and popping:** there is no terrain raster yet, so there are no seams. Labels do change at the band boundaries by design: Smolensk's two parts get their own labels at the local zoom, and on a small map markers drop their names.
