## Verification status (applies to every claim)

- **Verified:** supported by at least two independent, reputable sources, at least one of them scholarly or primary. Only verified facts are presented as documented history.
- **Disputed:** reputable sources disagree. Shown as a range, with the sources.
- **Corroborated:** several secondary or tertiary sources agree, but no scholarly or primary source has been checked yet. Shown in the game like Unverified.
- **Unverified:** from a single or a tertiary source. It may guide design work but is never presented as fact.
- Game data refers to claims by their ID in HISTORICAL_ASSUMPTIONS.md. An unverified item can enter the game only as a labelled game assumption.

## Minard's chart

Minard's *Carte figurative* (1869) is a celebrated graphic, drawn decades after the campaign from published accounts. Its figures are approximate and contested. We use it for its visual form, and as one cited source with caveats — never as precise or daily troop-strength data. The player's own "Minard" in the report is drawn from simulation data. The historical comparison band uses researched ranges at a few verified, dated waypoints.

**Design notes drawn from this (labelled as design, not history)**
- The Russian offensive of 7–8 August, stopped by a false report, is direct precedent for a Russian AI that acts on its own imperfect intelligence (§2.8, §2.9). The bait-and-converge approach (§2.13) rests on the same episode.
- The crossing at Rasasna and the delay at Krasnyi are the historical form of the southern swing. Taking the Old Town without holding the north bank — the historical result — becomes a geographic fact of the map, not a script.
