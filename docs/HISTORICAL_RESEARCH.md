## Verification status (applies to every claim)

- **Verified:** supported by at least two independent, reputable sources, at least one of them scholarly or primary. Only verified facts are presented as documented history.
- **Disputed:** reputable sources disagree. Shown as a range, with the sources.
- **Corroborated:** several secondary or tertiary sources agree, but no scholarly or primary source has been checked yet. Shown in the game like Unverified.
- **Unverified:** from a single or a tertiary source. It may guide design work but is never presented as fact.
- Game data refers to claims by their ID in HISTORICAL_ASSUMPTIONS.md. An unverified item can enter the game only as a labelled game assumption.

## Minard's chart

Minard's *Carte figurative* (1869) is a celebrated graphic, drawn decades after the campaign from published accounts. Its figures are approximate and contested. We use it for its visual form, and as one cited source with caveats — never as precise or daily troop-strength data. The player's own "Minard" in the report is drawn from simulation data. The historical comparison band uses researched ranges at a few verified, dated waypoints.

**Strengths and logistics: first pass (31 October 2026)**

- **Strengths (H-009, H-014):** the French central group around Smolensk in mid-August is most often put at about 180,000, and the combined Russian armies at about 120,000–130,000. Neither is well sourced online. Corps figures move fast: Ney's III Corps fell from about 44,000 in June to about 22,000–30,000 by mid-August. Starting strengths therefore enter the game as **ranges per force group**, labelled as such, until checked against Nafziger's or Riehn's tables.
- **Logistics rates (L-001 to L-006):** usable *ranges*, but no reliable French ration regulation or wagon specification was found online. The working figures are in the register.
- **The key finding for the design: fodder is heavy.** A horse's day of fodder (~9 kg) weighs about twelve times a man's daily ration (~0.55–0.75 kg). A wagon can carry well over a thousand man-days of food, but only about a hundred horse-days of fodder. The army's horses therefore had to live off the land: grazing, green crops, requisitioned hay. That is one historical root of the mass horse losses. **The rules currently treat a food unit and a fodder unit as the same weight; they should not** (see GAMEPLAY_BALANCING.md, rule values).

**Hunger, straggling and foraging (31 October 2026)**

The question: how fast should hunger wear an army down, and how much can it gather on the march? The first balance batches showed every advancing army collapsing from hunger within the 20 days of the scenario. This pass looked for primary figures and for modern evidence on underfeeding.

- **The advance cost a third of the army without a major battle (H-014).** Clausewitz, citing Chambray's figures:
  - The central army crossed the Niemen on 24 June with 301,000 men and stood at 182,000 at Smolensk on 15 August, after detaching 13,500.
  - At most 10,000 of the losses came in action. About 95,000 were lost to sickness and straggling in 52 days, roughly 1/150 of the original strength a day at first and 1/120 later.
  - That army then fought at Smolensk, Valutino and Borodino. It was worn, not broken.
- **The halts let stragglers return (H-017).** The army halted about 14 days at Vilna and about 11 at Vitebsk, "during which periods many stragglers had time to rejoin". The game does not model returning stragglers yet.
- **Hunger was chronic, not total (H-015).** Russian interrogations of prisoners, reported by Clausewitz, found that around Vitebsk:
  - the horses had only green forage;
  - the men were issued flour instead of bread and cooked it into soup;
  - the Guard was the exception.

  Rose's medical history, drawn from memoirs, adds dysentery from the Niemen onwards and men going a day or more without food, then marauding. It also adds Westphalian battalions halved on the advance, horses dying by the hundred on green fodder, and executions of marauders.
- **The Russians burned what they left and swept the fields; their own army ate well (H-016).** Clausewitz notes that the Russians "generally set fire" to their magazines and to the towns and villages they abandoned. He describes French foragers working far afield for scanty returns, because the population was thin and side roads few. The Russian army had biscuit, grits, meat, brandy and hay in plenty, and lost few men to sickness or straggling.
- **Living off the land works only in populated country (L-007).** Clausewitz's rules of thumb in *On War*:
  - A peasant household holds bread for 8–14 days.
  - Quarters never occupied before can feed three or four times their inhabitants for several days.
  - In moderately populated country (2,000–3,000 people per German square mile, about 36–54 per km²), an army of 150,000 can live on a continuous march without magazines.
  - An army is seldom short in the first three or four weeks of a campaign.

  The Vitebsk–Smolensk country fell well short of that, and the retreating Russians stripped it further.
- **Underfeeding takes weeks to sap a soldier's strength (L-008).** The US Institute of Medicine's 1995 review found:
  - Soldiers keep normal work capacity through short periods (under 10 days) of severely restricted food.
  - Strength losses are probably not seen below about 5% loss of body weight, and aerobic capacity falls from about 10%.
  - In the Ranger course, a deficit of 1,000–1,200 kcal a day cost 13–16% of body weight and about a fifth of maximal strength over eight weeks.

  Applying modern soldiers to 1812 is an inference. But it points the same way as the history: in a campaign of weeks, hunger's quick effects are men leaving the ranks (sickness, straggling, marauding), not the units that stay together losing their will to fight.

**What this means for the rules (G-017; the cohesion value was adopted in rules 0.7.0, D-030):**
- **Cohesion loss from hunger is far too harsh at −100 a day at a full shortfall.** The model's straggling (2% a day at a full shortfall) plus sickness (0.2%) reproduce the historical loss rate at an average shortfall of about a quarter to a third. At that shortfall the present rule takes 24–32 cohesion a day, so an army at 800 would reach the collapse line in two and a half to three and a half weeks. The real army marched seven and a half weeks and still fought.
  - Values of about **20–60 a day** fit both the advance and the retreat, where two to three weeks of hunger, cold and pursuit did break the army.
  - **30** sits at the ceiling set by the strictest reading of the advance, which assumes hunger on every day, halts included.
- **Straggling at 2% a day at a full shortfall fits** the historical loss rate. Keep it (range 1–3%).
- **Foraging on the march, at 0.2 rations a man a day, sits at the low end of what is plausible** (range 0.1–0.5). That fits thin population, burned villages and an army massed on one road. Keep it, and show its sensitivity.
- **Returning stragglers** are worth modelling later: they are why halts restored the army historically.

- **The Moscow road.** Clausewitz describes Barclay leaving by the Porechye road and regaining the Moscow road near Lubino by a side road, a vulnerable flank march. That supports Valutino and Lubino as a place for an interception; whether they are the only places that cut the line of retreat is not established.

No new threshold follows from these few cases.

## Battles and the arms of the armies (2 November 2026)

Researched for the sector battles (§2.10; TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE §3.4b). Register entries H-020, H-021, L-012 and G-021.

**What the summer's battles cost (H-020).** These figures are context, not calibration targets. The battles differ in kind: a rearguard fight at Krasnyi, the storm of a walled city at Smolensk, a rearguard action at Valutino, a pitched battle at Borodino. Their losses are shares of the troops engaged, not of the armies.
- **Krasnyi, 14 August:** Neverovsky's 6,000–7,000 (up to 1,500 of them cavalry, 14 guns) lost about 1,500 men and 7 guns to about 20,000 French (Murat's 15,000 cavalry and 5,000 of Ney's infantry), who lost about 500 (Bodart 1908; Riehn 1990). Most of the division got away.
- **Smolensk, 16–18 August:** about 45,000 of 180,000 French and 30,000 of 120,000 Russians engaged; losses 9,000–10,000 French and 6,000–14,000 Russian, the authors differing widely (Bodart; Chandler; Mikaberidze). Barclay left the city, destroying stores and bridges, and covered the retreat with a rearguard on the far bank.
- **Valutino, 19 August:** about 35,000 French against 25,000 Russians; losses 7,000–8,800 French and about 6,000 Russian (Bodart; Palmer 2003). Most of Barclay's army had already got away.
- **Borodino, 7 September:** about 110,000–128,000 French and 122,000–155,000 Russians engaged; losses 30,000–42,000 French and 40,000–53,000 Russian. The Russian army left the next day in two columns (Riehn; Zamoyski 2004; Mikaberidze 2007). This is the negative control: heavy losses and lost ground, yet an orderly withdrawal.

**Artillery and cavalry (H-021, L-012).** At Borodino the French had 587 guns and 317 squadrons among about 128,000 men; the Guard alone 109 guns and 27 squadrons among about 18,500 (Riehn; Mikaberidze). The Russians had 637 guns, 164 squadrons and 20 Cossack regiments. In June 1812 the 1st Western Army counted 104,250 men and 7,000 Cossacks with 558 guns, the 2nd 33,000 and 4,000 Cossacks with 216 guns (Smith 1998). A gun team took 6–8 horses, and a French gun moved with about one caisson of its 2–5, at 4–6 horses each.

**What the game takes from this (G-021).** Cavalry and gun ranges for each group, anchored on these figures where they exist (Murat's cavalry, the Guard's and Bagration's guns, Neverovsky's division) and otherwise set by the armies' ratios of guns to men. The cavalry of the infantry corps and the Guard's quality are our estimates.

**Not obtained.** Nafziger's dated unit returns for 1 and 15 August 1812 (812HAB, 812HDA) refused automated access. They are the next step for the composition, unit by unit.

## Roads east of Smolensk (2 November 2026)

Researched after the pinning bot showed that no Russian army at Smolensk's north bank can be shut in on the present network (GAMEPLAY_BALANCING, "Pinning"). Register entry H-022.

Edward Foord's *Napoleon's Russian Campaign of 1812* (1914), chapter V, describes the roads round the city. Three roads left the north gate, the Moscow road running some 5 miles along the Dnieper before crossing the chord of a great bend to Solovievo. "A cross-road passed by the village of Gorbunovo in a shallow curve to the high-road at Lubino, 14 miles distant", and Barclay's main columns took that track on the night of 18–19 August. "From the south side of Smolensk another track crossed the Dnieper by a ford at Prudichevo, about 6 miles out", and joined the high road about 2 miles west of Lubino; on the 19th Napoleon sent Eblé to bridge it and Junot's corps to cross there. The Wikipedia summary of Valutino agrees on Junot and Prudichevo.

**What the game takes from it:** the Prudichevo track as a candidate road, not yet adopted (MAP_AND_GEOGRAPHY.md). The Gorbunovo cross-road leaves from beside the north-bank suburb and adds no new way out on this network.

**Still to do:** check both against Clausewitz's account of Barclay's flank march (the PDF is too large to fetch here) and a period map; neither Prudichevo nor this Gorbunovo has a Wikidata location.

## The third review's history audit (3 November 2026)

The reviewer found three historical claims to check: the Prudichevo crossing (H-022), the dated French returns (H-021, G-021) and the scopes of the battle figures (H-020). No rule changed.

**The Prudichevo crossing (H-022): supported, and the map's compression is small.**
- Foord's text, read again with its page numbers: the ford "about 6 miles out" and the junction with the high road "about 2 miles west of Lubino" (p. 131); on the 19th Napoleon "sent Eblé to Prudichevo to bridge the Dnieper" and ordered Junot to cross there, and a column was seen crossing that morning (p. 148); Junot advanced from Prudichevo during the action (p. 152).
- **A second account agrees.** The Russian *Military Encyclopaedia* (Sytin, vol. 5, 1911, pp. 220–221, article "Valutina gora"; public domain, dates in the Old Style) has Russian outposts reporting French bridge-building on the Dnieper "about 10 versts above Smolensk, near Prudishchevo" (about 10.7 km upstream, matching Foord's 6 miles), Junot crossing at Prudishchevo, and Murat's cavalry fording the river beside it.
- **Where the track met the high road.** Lubino (Wikidata Q13635925) lies about 3.5 km north-east of the game's Valutino, so a junction about 2 miles (3.2 km) west of Lubino falls within about a kilometre of Valutino. The game's road ends where the track did, to within the network's precision. From the Old Town it is 12.6 km by the network rule; the track ran about 10 km to the ford and a few more to the junction, so the real way was somewhat longer, and the game marches it at 42% of a post road's pace (a track, and a ford).
- **What the sources do not settle:** whether infantry and guns could ford it before Eblé's bridge (the cavalry did on the 19th), and whether anyone used it before the 19th. The game lets either side use it throughout, slowly. A village of that name could not be found in present-day registers; Clausewitz and a period map are still unread.

**The French returns of 1 August (H-021, G-021): obtained, totalled, not adopted.** Nafziger's order of battle of the Grande Armée for 1 August 1812 (collection no. 812HAB, 30 pages, citing Fabry, *Campagne de Russie*), from the collection Nafziger donated to the US Army's Combined Arms Research Library, was read from the Internet Archive's copy of the library's page. We use its figures, not its text. - **The totals do not fit mid-August.** The central army is usually put at about 180,000 in mid-August (H-009); this return gives 262,600 for the same groups on 1 August. Two weeks of attrition might explain part of it; the rest suggests that some figures are earlier states carried forward. The return names Fabry but not the date of each figure. The 15 August return (812HDA, 17 pages) is the check, and was not read in this audit.
- **Cavalry is the largest gap.** The vanguard's 13,000–17,000 came from the 15,000 Murat had at Krasnyi on 14 August (H-021); the return gives his three corps and Ney's light cavalry 30,400 two weeks earlier. Horses died fast that summer, so both may be right, each at its date.
- **Guns differ less, and both ways.** The Guard has more field guns in the return (154, with line horse companies attached to its reserve) than at Borodino (109); Davout, Eugène and the southern group have fewer field and regimental guns than the game gives them (the game's were set at 4.6 guns per 1,000 men).
- **Not adopted.** Any change to the ranges changes battles, and the date of the figures is in doubt. Read the 15 August return first, then propose ranges with measured effects.

- **Applying each formation's change to its 1 August total** (VIII Corps at the overall rate, since the later return omits it) gives about **203,700 for the five groups in mid-August**. That is inside the game's 162,000–213,000 and near the 180,000 usually given (H-009). So the 1 August return fits its date, and the game's ranges fit the middle of the month.
- **But the game starts on 5 August.** Between the two returns, the same groups would have stood at about **246,000** on 5 August (a straight line between 262,600 and 203,700). The game's Russians start at their early-August strength (121,000 on 6 August, Foord p. 118, inside the game's 121,000–144,000), while its French start at about their mid-August strength: some three quarters of what the returns give for that day. The game's own hunger and sickness then take the French lower still.
- **The vanguard's cavalry** would be about 22,000–23,000 in mid-August by the same reckoning, against the game's 13,000–17,000 at the start.
- **The options were:** keep the French ranges as they are (they follow the usual mid-August figure, and the balance rests on them); measure a start at the 5 August strengths as a what-if first, then decide; or correct only the composition (the vanguard's cavalry).

**The battle figures (H-020): re-cited with their scopes.** Foord (1914) gives page-cited figures that differ from the encyclopedias' in what they count:
- **Krasnyi, 14 August:** Neverovsky's square of about 5,000 (9 battalions, his cavalry and 8 guns behind the ravine, a battalion and 2 guns in the town) lost about 1,500 men, half of them prisoners, and 8 guns; the French loss "did not appear to exceed 800" (pp. 127–128). No French strength is given.
- **Smolensk, 16–17 August:** on the 16th Raevsky held the city with "scarcely 13,000 men" and 72 guns, his own count, probably infantry and artillery only (p. 131). On the 17th about 19,000 Russians faced the assault of some 55,000 French infantry (p. 140), out of over 120,000 French before the city (p. 138); Dokhturov's whole force "probably hardly exceeded 30,000", 55 battalions engaged (p. 143); the two Russian armies counted 121,000 with some 650 guns before the operations (p. 118). Losses: about 6,000 Russian on the 17th and 7,000 for both days, about 12,000 French (pp. 143–144).
- **Valutino, 19 August:** about 50,000 French on the field, "perhaps 37,000" seriously engaged, against "probably about 30,000" Russians brought in one after another; losses over 7,000 French and about 5,000 Russian, prisoners "not more than some hundreds" (p. 155).
- **Each figure is a share of something different:** a day's fighting, a corps, the troops engaged, or the army. The register now says which.

**A local rearguard as a control.** The reviewer asked for a local rearguard action and a closed exit to be tested apart. On the engine's test ground, a division of 6,500 (1,200 cavalry, 14 guns) caught in the open by 20,000 (15,000 cavalry), with its road open, got away in order in 52 of 60 sets of dice and routed in 8, always by its own road, losing a median 6.8% of its men and 1 gun. Neverovsky also got away, but lost about a quarter of his men and half his guns. The game lets such a rearguard off more lightly than Krasnyi did; that is recorded, not tuned (no band is a target). With every road out held, the same division never got away in order. Both are now tests (`third-review.test.ts`, section 7).
