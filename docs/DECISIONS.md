| D-040 | 2026-11-01 | **Rules 0.15.0: supply issued by need, judged day by day, and forecasts that follow the movement rules** (second independent review, findings B3, C3 and the time-aware part of finding 2). A wagon train hands over what the group would draw at a depot, and the rest stays on the train. Both issue as much food as the group can carry, two days' fodder and its ammunition (G-020), and only what it could still march with; they reckon that room the way a marching group sheds its load, fodder first, so held fodder never blocks food. Measuring this showed that the earlier results relied on a loophole: a corps at its depot drew four days' food from it, and its own train then loaded more there and handed it over, so corps set out with full wagons and one could hoard while another starved. With the loophole closed, a four-day issue (a placeholder never registered) tripled the marching armies' collapses; issuing all a corps can carry, the researched carrying rule, did not. The project owner chose to issue up to what a corps can carry. "Sustainable" is checked day by day: a train's food counts from the day it can arrive, and a group in battle forages nothing. The staff's arrival forecast runs the engine's own hourly movement for the force alone, with fatigue, night rest and gathering; the wet-weather bound is now mud on every road from tomorrow | Trains delivered whatever they carried, so stocks ended very uneven; "sustainable" counted food that would arrive after the day it was needed; forecasts used separate arithmetic and could be a sub-step out | User approval ("Yes, supply next"); the issue level by the user's decision ("Up to what it can carry") |
